## Platform

web

## Stack

Astro 7 (already scaffolded in this repository, Node >= 22.12). Static output. The
incumbent public site at jetlinetransport.ca is a separate client-rendered React SPA
generated by Hostinger Horizons; it is being replaced, not migrated.

## Users

Primary: **airline and aviation operations coordinators** — crew schedulers, FBO staff,
charter and flight-department dispatchers — who must move pilots and cabin crew between
aircraft and hotels on a clock they do not control. Their job is to make crew rest and
report times hold when a flight slips. They evaluate a ground provider on whether it
absorbs irregularity without being managed.

Secondary: **corporate travel managers and executive assistants** booking point-to-point
executive travel, airport transfers, roadshows, and standing employee shuttle programs in
the Greater Toronto Area. They are buying predictability and one accountable contact.

Tertiary: **executive and VIP passengers** themselves, who judge the service in the
30 seconds between a terminal door and a vehicle.

Both primary and secondary audiences are professional repeat buyers, not one-time
consumers. They arrive already knowing what they need; the site's job is to establish
that Jetline is a credible operator and to open a conversation.

## Product Purpose

Jetline Executive Ground Transportation moves airline crews, corporate travellers, and
executives around the Greater Toronto Area on schedules driven by aviation. Success for
the website is a qualified inbound request — a crew contract, a corporate account, or an
executive transfer — from someone who understood within seconds that Jetline is an
aviation-fluent operator rather than a limousine company.

## Positioning

Jetline is built around aviation timing rather than adapted to it. 25+ years in executive
ground transportation with 6+ years of it spent specifically on airline crew operations at
Billy Bishop Toronto City Airport — the differentiator is operational fluency in how flights
actually behave (delays, diversions, crew rest, planeside protocol), not vehicle luxury.
A neighbouring limousine company cannot truthfully claim the crew-operations history.

## Operating Context

- Work is dispatched against flight schedules, not calendar appointments. Pickup times move
  when aircraft move.
- Crew rest and duty-time rules make lateness a compliance problem for the client, not just
  an inconvenience.
- Demand concentrates at night and in early morning — crew hotel runs, first departures,
  late arrivals.
- Airside/FBO work follows airport and operator protocol rather than curbside pickup.
- Corporate work is contract- and account-shaped: recurring shuttles, standing accounts,
  named points of contact.
- There is **no online booking or availability system.** All work begins as a request that
  a human quotes and confirms. The site must never imply instant booking.

## Product Principles

1. **Aviation fluency is the whole argument.** Every page should read as though written by
   someone who has moved a crew at 04:30 during a diversion. Vehicle luxury is secondary.
2. **Never publish an unverified fact.** No phone, no address, no testimonial, no statistic,
   no vehicle model, no client name beyond what is recorded above. The incumbent site
   already shipped a fake phone number; that class of error must not repeat.
3. **Sell certainty, not cars.** Timing, coordination, discretion, and reliability are the
   product. The fleet is an instrument.
4. **The request is the conversion, not a booking.** Every CTA opens a human conversation.
5. **Professional buyers, minimal copy.** The audience is expert and time-poor. Say less,
   and make the practical facts unmissable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific standard was established by the user. Treat WCAG 2.2 AA as the working
floor: the site is photography-heavy and dark-toned, so text-over-image contrast, visible
focus states, keyboard-operable navigation, meaningful alt text, and `prefers-reduced-motion`
support are the load-bearing requirements.
